Elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) is capable of providing high security than to other cryptosystems with 
same key size. The aim of this paper is twofold. Firstly, we present new methods for the construction 
of substitution boxes (S-boxes), and the generation of pseudo random numbers (PRN) by using a total 
order on an elliptic curve (EC) over a prime ﬁeld. A search method is used to eﬃciently generate an EC 
instead of the more traditional group law which is computationally expensive. The S-box generation tech- 
nique uses the x -coordinates of the points of an ordered elliptic curve (OEC), while a generalization of 
the Frobenius map and n -norm are used on the points of an OEC to generate PRN. Secondly, a two phase 
image encryption system based on the newly developed S-box and PRN generation methods is proposed. 
In this security system, the plain-image is ﬁrst diffused by masking it by the proposed PRN which is 
then confused by a proposed dynamic S-box. Rigorous analysis and comparison with some of the exist- 
ing S-box and image encryption methods reveal that the proposed techniques are capable of generating 
cryptographically strong S-boxes, PRN with high entropy and optimal resistance against modern image 
cryptanalysis.

1. Introduction 
Security of conﬁdential data has received much attention by 
cryptographers in the past few decades. Different types of infor- 
mation security techniques have been proposed by the researchers. 
Cryptography and steganography are the two main categories of 
the information security techniques [1] . The main idea of crypto- 
graphic techniques is to transform conﬁdential data into an un- 
readable form by using key(s). In steganography, the secret data 
is secured from adversaries by embedding it into host data. Shan- 
non [2] proved that for a strong security system it is necessary 
to generate confusion and diffusion in the data up to a certain 
level. There are different types of cryptosystems based on differ- 
ent mathematical structures. In many cryptographic techniques, an 
S-box is solely responsible for confusion and diffusion in the in- 
put data. Similarly, elliptic curves (ECs) are also used in developing 
secure cryptosystems. Miller ﬁrst time introduced a cryptosystem 
based on ECs in [3] which is 20 percent faster than Diﬃe-Hellmans 
protocol. Another fast and secure cryptosystem is proposed by Neal 
et al. in [4] based on the concept of a discrete logarithmic problem 
on ECs. Amara et al. [5] compared ECC with RSA and noticed that 
ECC has higher security than that of RSA.

The aim of this paper is to present novel and eﬃcient algo- 
rithms for the construction of cryptographically strong S-boxes, 
pseudo random numbers (PRN) with high entropy, and security of 
conﬁdential images based on elliptic curves over prime ﬁelds. The 
newly developed S-box technique uses the x -coordinates of points 
of a ﬁnite OEC followed by modulo operation 256. The proposed 
PRN generation scheme is based on a generalization of the Frobe- 
nius map, and n -norm over a ﬁnite OEC, while the proposed image 
cryptosystem is based on a dynamic S-box and PRN generated by 
the newly developed methods. 
The rest of the paper is organized as follow: Section 2 contains 
some preliminaries. A survey of existing S-box generation tech- 
niques is given in Section 3. A new algorithm for the generation 
of S-boxes over ﬁnite OEC, their analysis and comparison are pre- 
sented in Section 4 . Section 5 contains the details and analysis of 
proposed PRN generation method. A short survey of existing im- 
age encryption techniques is given in Section 6 . Section 7 contains 
a new image encryption system based on the proposed S-box and 
PRN generation techniques. Moreover, a detailed analysis and com- 
parison of the newly developed image security system is presented 
in Section 7 . Finally, conclusion and future directions are discussed 
in Section 8 .

3. A brief introduction to S-box construction techniques 
The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) has 
adopted the Rijndael block cipher [7] as the Advanced Encryption 
Standard (AES). AES is one of the most preferred cryptosystems. 
Due to the signiﬁcance of AES many cryptographers have explored 
the cryptographic properties of its S-box. Murphy et al. calculated 
an algebraic representation of the AES S-box in [8] . They also ob- 
served that AES can be represented by a system of quadratic equa- 
tions. The non-linear part of AES is further investigated by Rosen- 
thal in [9] by examining its polynomial representation. It is con- 
cluded that the AES S-box has low algebraic complexity because 
it has few non-zero terms in its linear polynomial, and hence AES 
might be vulnerable against algebraic attacks. 
As an S-box plays a vital role in AES, therefore many researchers 
have suggested its improvements using various mathematical con- 
cepts including but not limited to algebraic and differential equa- 
tions. Lui et al. [11] upgraded the AES S-box by swapping the or- 
ders of the transformations of the AES S-box. Cui et al. [12] de- 
ployed an aﬃne function to enhance the complexity of the AES 
S-box against various algebraic attacks such as algebraic complex- 
ity. Gray codes have been used in [13] with the AES S-box to 
maximum the number of non-zero terms. Khan et al. [14,15] used 
group action, orbit of power function and aﬃne mappings to gen- 
eralise Gray S-box to construct multiple S-boxes. Hussain et al. 
[16] used Mobius transformations to construct new S-boxes. Guop- 
ing et al. [17] designed new S-boxes using logistic and the Baker 
maps. A new method using Baker and Chebyshev maps is pre- 
sented in [18] by Guo et al. Wang et al. [19] constructed new S- 
boxes using artiﬁcial neural networks and a chaotic map . Jung 
et al. [20] explored a relationship between the non-linearity of 
S-box and hyper-elliptic curves. Hayat et al. [21] proposed a con- 
struction of S-boxes based on ﬁnite EC.

5. Pseudo random numbers (PRN) generation scheme 
Random numbers and their generation play a vital role in many 
cryptographic applications such as encryption, key generation, in- 
ternet gambling and masking protocols. Several random number 
generation methods are proposed based on different mathemati- 
cal structures. ECs are also used for the generation of PRN [30–33] . 
Usually, the EC based PRN generation methods use the group law 
for the generation of points on an EC which requires high com- 
putation effort. In this section, we propose an eﬃcient method to 
generate PRN over an OEC based on a search method, a generaliza- 
tion of the Frobenius map, and an n -norm.

6. A brief introduction to image encryption techniques 
Security of conﬁdential images has become one of the hot areas 
in cryptography. The traditional security systems, particularly, the 
cryptosystems relying on only a single S-box are not good enough 
to ensure the security of images [54] . This is because of the differ- 
ent nature of images compared to text in terms of high correlation 
among adjacent pixels, high data capacity and hence high redun- 
dancy. It is therefore necessary to develop some new cryptosys- 
tems which can eﬃciently resist the image security attacks. Many

396 U. Hayat, N.A. Azam / Signal Processing 155 (2019) 391–402 
Fig. 1. The ﬁrst 200 random numbers of different random number sets generated by the proposed algorithm. 
researchers have proposed different image cryptosystems. Most of 
the time these cryptosystems can be divided into three categories: 
(a) Sometimes multiple S-boxes are used instead of a single S- 
box for diffusion and confusion of secret images e.g., see [35] and 
references therein. Usually, with the increase in the number of 
multiple S-boxes not only the security but also the computation 
time increases. Thus, in this area the interest lies in reducing the 
number of S-boxes to achieve the desired security. 
(b) In the second category, PRN are used for the security of 
images. These PRN are generated by different methods. In some 
cryptosystems, it is necessary to send all PRN as key for decryp- 
tion. This increases the security of images but the drawback of 
such techniques is the increase in the data size. However, some 
cryptosystems require a few number of parameters to generate the 
random numbers. Such techniques include chaos and elliptic curve 
based random number generation methods. In chaos based random 
number generation, the interest lies in the selection of a highly 
chaotic system. Different types of chaotic maps such as Arnold’s 
cat map, Lorenz system, Baker’s map, and Chen chaotic attractor 
are used for the generation of random numbers. However, in the 
case of elliptic curve based random sequence, the main focus is on 
the method of generation of points on the EC. Usually, the group 
law on the EC is used for this purpose. The cryptographic eﬃciency 
of random numbers is highly dependent on the period of the un- 
derlying systems. That is, a sequence of random numbers is crypto- 
graphically strong if it has high period. However, chaotic sequences 
can have short periods depending on the nature of computational 
accuracy. Furthermore, in general the EC cryptography has high se- 
curity compared to the chaos based cryptography [36] . This is be- 
cause of the EC discrete logarithm problem is NP hard [37,38] . For 
more details on cryptography and random numbers the readers are 
referred to [33,36,39–41,43,51,52] . 
(c) The third category of image cryptosystems contains hybrid 
security systems based on S-boxes and PRN. Once again chaotic 
maps and elliptic curves are used in these techniques for the gen- 
eration of random numbers. The number of S-boxes used in these 
techniques varies. Of course, the time complexity of a hybrid im- 
age encryption technique is directly proportional to the number of 
S-boxes it uses. Furthermore, some techniques use static S-boxes 
while others use dynamic S-boxes, see [30,45,46,49,53] . With some 
calculations it can be shown that the techniques using dynamic S- 
boxes are more secure than those using static S-boxes, since all 
images are not of the same nature [47] . However, to the best of 
our knowledge, until now no EC cryptosystem has been developed 
based on the concept of dynamic S-boxes.

7.3.4. Key analysis 
Key attacks are commonly used by the cryptanalyst to break the 
security of a cryptosystem. Thus for a secure security system, it is 
important to have high key strength. Key spacing, key sensitivity 
and known plain-text and chosen plain-text tests are commonly 
used for key analysis. A brief description of these tests and exper- 
imental results of the proposed cryptosystem are given below. 
1. Key spacing analysis: The resistance of a security technique 
against a brute-force attack is measured by its key spacing. In 
general, a cryptosystem satisﬁes the key space analysis if it has 
key spacing more than 2 128 . In the proposed security technique, 
nine keys p , a , b , q , n , p ′ , a ′ , b ′ and ℓ are used as explained in 
decryption section. We represent each of these keys by 512-bits. 
This implies that the key spacing of the proposed algorithm is 
much larger than 2 128 . Hence, the proposed encryption tech- 
nique resists brute-force attacks eﬃciently.

7.4. Comparison and discussion 
A comparison of the proposed encryption system and some of 
the existing cryptosystems based on different mathematical struc- 
tures including EC and chaos presented in [30,42–45,48–51] is con- 
ducted in this section. All security tests are applied on the listed 
security system by using the Lena image of size 256 ×256. The ex- 
perimental results are presented in Table 12 . 
It is evident from Table 12 that the security results of the 
proposed encryption technique are comparable with the different 
type of encryption algorithms. The technique in [30] uses a PRN 
generated by the group law on an EC while the proposed tech- 
nique uses a search method to generate random numbers on an 
OEC. Hence the random number scheme in [30] takes more time 
than the proposed scheme, since the group law method involves 
more operations than the search method. Furthermore, the secu- 
rity system in [30] uses AES as a static S-box which makes this 
scheme vulnerable against algebraic attacks and data analysis at- 
tacks as discussed in Sections 3 and 6 . However, the proposed 
encryption system uses a single dynamic S-box generated by the 
points on an OEC which has high algebraic complexity as shown 
in Table 2 . Hence, the proposed S-box is more secure than the 
technique in [30] . The scheme in [48] uses random numbers gen- 
erated by chaotic maps. Thus, the proposed scheme is eﬃcient 
than the scheme in [48] , since the random numbers generated by 
an EC are more secure than the PRN generated by chaotic maps 
[36] . In a similar way to [30] , the cryptosystem in [48] uses one 
static S-box for encryption of all type of images and requires mul- 
tiple rounds to achieve the standard security level. But the pro- 
posed scheme uses dynamic S-box whose generation is linked with 
the plain-image by using Equations (15) , (16), (18) and (19) , and 
satisﬁes all security tests in a single round. This makes the pro- 
posed algorithm fast and more robust than [48] . Similarly, the

8. Conclusion 
A hybrid image encryption technique based on a dynamic S- 
box, and pseudo random numbers (PRN) over an ordered elliptic 
curve (OEC) is proposed in this article. The x -coordinates of the 
OEC followed by modulo 256 operation are used for the generation 
of S-boxes, while the concepts of a generalized Frobenius map, and 
n -norm are used on an OEC for the generation of PRN. The newly 
developed image cryptosystem ﬁrst masks the plain-image by us- 
ing the proposed PRN, and then creates confusion in the masked 
image by using a dynamic proposed S-box. 
An interesting future problem will be to ﬁnd a method to en- 
crypt a color image with only a single round of the proposed algo- 
rithm without increasing the computation complexity.
